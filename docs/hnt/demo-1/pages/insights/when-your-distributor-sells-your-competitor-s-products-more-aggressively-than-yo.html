When Your Distributor Sells Your Competitor's Products More Aggressively Than Yours

By Ajit Jain · 2 min read

A distributor may represent several brands and naturally focus on the products that are easier to sell, offer better margins or receive stronger customer demand. If your products are not getting attention, blaming the distributor will not solve the problem.

Understand what drives their priorities. Is your product range competitive? Are stocks available? Does the distributor's sales team understand the applications? Are margins and schemes attractive? Is your company supporting leads, training and technical service?

Meet the distributor to review actual performance by product, customer and territory. Identify a few specific growth opportunities and agree on joint actions. This could include customer visits, application training, demonstrations or a focused campaign. Make commitments on both sides measurable.

Also assess whether the distributor has the capability and willingness to grow your business. If the relationship remains inactive despite support, consider additional channels or a different partner.

The takeawayA distributor relationship is a business partnership. Both parties must see a reason to invest effort, and both must be accountable for agreed outcomes.

— Ajit

Facing this in your business?

Strengthen channel strategy, partner selection, distributor performance and joint growth planning.
