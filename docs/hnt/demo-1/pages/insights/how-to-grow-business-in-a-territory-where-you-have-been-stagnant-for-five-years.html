Winning Customers

How to Grow Business in a Territory Where You Have Been Stagnant for Five Years

By Ajit Jain · 2 min read

A territory that has shown little growth for five years can become comfortable and frustrating at the same time. The salesperson knows the regular customers, understands the competition and has a predictable routine. But the same approach may no longer be enough to create new business.

Start with a fresh territory diagnosis. Which industries are growing? Which plants are expanding or investing? Are there new applications for your products? Have customer needs changed? Are competitors serving accounts that you have never seriously approached?

Segment the market rather than treating every prospect equally. Identify a small number of high-potential accounts and build a specific entry plan for each. Look beyond the purchase department to production, engineering, maintenance and management. Ask existing customers where else your solution could be useful.

Set measurable objectives for new accounts, new applications and share of wallet. Review progress monthly and change the approach when evidence suggests it is not working.

The takeawayA stagnant territory does not always need a new salesperson. It may need a new view of the market and a more deliberate growth plan.

— Ajit

Facing this in your business?

Build a more disciplined sales engine, from opportunity creation and qualification through conversion, forecasting and account development.
