How to Sell a High-Value Industrial Product With a Long Payback Period

By Ajit Jain · 2 min read

A high-value industrial product may offer better efficiency or lower operating costs, but the customer may hesitate because the initial investment is large and the payback takes time. A presentation full of technical features will not always help the customer secure approval.

Start with the customer's financial and operational priorities. What is the current cost of running the process? What losses occur through downtime, energy use, maintenance, rejects or labour? Build a comparison between the current situation and the proposed solution, using realistic assumptions and customer data wherever possible.

Show the payback period, but also explain the risks. What happens if production volumes change? How reliable are the savings estimates? Can the investment be introduced in phases or tested at one location first?

Involve finance and operations early so the proposal answers their questions. Avoid overstating benefits to make the numbers look attractive.

The takeawayCustomers invest when they believe the business case and trust the supplier's ability to deliver. A credible financial story is as important as a technically strong product.

— Ajit
