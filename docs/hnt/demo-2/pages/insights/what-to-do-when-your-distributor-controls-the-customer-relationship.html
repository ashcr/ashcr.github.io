Channel & Distributors

What to Do When Your Distributor Controls the Customer Relationship

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

In some markets, the distributor owns most customer contacts, holds the buying history and decides which supplier gets access. This can make the manufacturer dependent on one partner for market information and customer continuity.

The answer is not to bypass the distributor or take over their customers. That can damage trust and create channel conflict. Instead, agree on how the manufacturer can add value to the relationship. Joint visits, technical workshops, application support and periodic business reviews can benefit the customer and the distributor.

Build a shared understanding of key accounts. Record relevant customer needs, open opportunities and agreed actions in a way both parties can use. Where appropriate, develop relationships with plant, engineering or quality teams while respecting the distributor's commercial role.

The manufacturer should also understand the distributor's business model and concerns. If the partner fears losing control, explain clearly how direct engagement will work.

The takeaway

A strong channel model combines the distributor's local access with the manufacturer's product and technical strengths. The customer should experience one coordinated team.
