Managing Sales Teams

When Your Best Salesperson Becomes Your Biggest Management Problem

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

A top salesperson brings in important orders, knows key customers and often enjoys considerable influence. But what happens when the same person ignores reporting, refuses to share information or behaves poorly with colleagues? Managers sometimes tolerate this because they fear losing the revenue.

That tolerance sends a message to the rest of the team: results matter more than behaviour. It can damage trust and make the organisation dependent on one individual. The answer is not to dismiss performance, but to treat performance and conduct as two separate expectations.

Have a direct, private conversation. Recognise the person's contribution, describe the specific behaviour that must change and explain its impact on customers and colleagues. Agree on clear expectations and review progress. Also reduce business dependency by strengthening customer relationships, documenting account information and developing backup contacts.

The takeaway

A strong performer deserves recognition, not immunity. Sustainable sales growth depends on results delivered in a way that the whole organisation can support.
