What Should a Salesperson Do When the Customer Stops Responding?

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

A customer who once responded quickly has suddenly gone quiet. Calls are unanswered, messages receive no reply and the proposal seems to have disappeared. Repeated follow-ups can make the salesperson appear desperate, but doing nothing may allow a genuine opportunity to slip away.

First, consider what may have changed. Has the project been delayed, has the contact moved roles, is there an internal approval issue or has the customer selected another supplier? Avoid assuming that silence means rejection.

Use a thoughtful follow-up. Refer to the specific discussion, offer a useful next step and make it easy for the customer to respond. For example, ask whether the project is still active and whether a revised timeline would help. If appropriate, contact another stakeholder without creating confusion or pressure.

After a reasonable number of attempts, move the opportunity to a suitable status and focus on other prospects. Keep the door open professionally.

The takeaway

Follow-up is important, but persistence should be guided by relevance. The aim is to help the customer make progress, not simply to remind them that you are waiting.
