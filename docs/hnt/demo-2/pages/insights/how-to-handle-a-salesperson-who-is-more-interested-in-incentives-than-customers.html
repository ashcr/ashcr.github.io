Managing Sales Teams

How to Handle a Salesperson Who Is More Interested in Incentives Than Customers

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

Incentives can energise a sales team, but they can also narrow attention. A salesperson may chase orders that close quickly, offer excessive discounts to meet a slab or ignore service issues that do not affect the immediate payout. The incentive plan may be producing the behaviour it was designed to reward, but not the behaviour the business actually needs.

Start by examining what the scheme measures. Does it reward only revenue, or also profitable growth, collections, new customers, product mix and customer retention? Are targets realistic and understood? Do people know how their actions affect their earnings?

Discuss the issue with the salesperson using actual examples. Explain that incentives are one part of performance, not a substitute for sound judgement. Review the scheme with the leadership team and correct measures that encourage unhealthy short-term decisions.

The takeaway

A good incentive plan should align personal achievement with business health. The objective is not to reduce motivation. It is to ensure that the energy created by incentives moves the organisation in the right direction.
