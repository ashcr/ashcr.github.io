When the Production Head Wants Your Product but the Purchase Department Rejects Your Price

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

This situation is common in manufacturing. The production head sees an operational benefit in your product, but purchase focuses on the quoted price and pushes for a lower offer. The salesperson can feel caught between two customer stakeholders.

Avoid treating purchase as an obstacle. Understand the measures each person is responsible for. Production may value reliability, output and reduced downtime. Purchase may be measured on savings, terms and supplier competitiveness. Both perspectives are legitimate.

Build a business case that connects the operational benefit to financial impact. If your product reduces stoppages or consumption, estimate the value using data the customer accepts. Present the comparison clearly and be transparent about assumptions. Invite both functions to review the evidence together.

If a price concession is still needed, consider what commitment the customer can offer in return. Do not give away margin without understanding the total opportunity.

The takeaway

The strongest proposal helps production justify the operational choice and helps purchase justify the commercial decision.
