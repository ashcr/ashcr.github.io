Winning Customers

The Customer Likes You, But Doesn't Give You the Order. What Are You Missing?

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

You have had several pleasant meetings. The customer responds to your calls, appreciates your product and says the relationship is good. Yet the order keeps going to someone else. This is one of the most frustrating situations in industrial selling.

The mistake is to confuse a good relationship with a buying decision. The customer may like you but see no strong reason to change suppliers. Your price may be uncompetitive, your technical advantage may not be clear, or the real decision-maker may not be involved. There may also be an unresolved concern that the customer is reluctant to discuss.

Ask better questions. What problem would make a change worthwhile? What would the customer need to see in a trial? Who else must approve the decision? What is preventing the next step? Try to understand the customer's business case, not just their personal opinion of you.

The takeaway

A relationship creates access and trust. A clear business reason creates an order. Successful salespeople work deliberately on both.
