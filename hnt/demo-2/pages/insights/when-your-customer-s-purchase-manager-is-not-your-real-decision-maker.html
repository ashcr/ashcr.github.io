Winning Customers

When Your Customer's Purchase Manager Is Not Your Real Decision-Maker

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

In industrial selling, the person who requests the quotation is not always the person who decides which product will be used. Purchase may control the commercial process, while production, maintenance, quality, engineering or finance influence the final choice.

If a salesperson builds a relationship only with purchase, they may discover too late that the technical team prefers another supplier or that the plant head has concerns about reliability. The reverse can also happen: production supports your product, but purchase rejects the price.

Map the decision-making group early. Ask who defines the specification, who evaluates performance, who approves the supplier and who releases the order. Meet these stakeholders with a message relevant to their responsibilities. Production may care about uptime; quality about consistency; purchase about total commercial value.

Do not try to bypass purchase or undermine anyone. The aim is to understand the full decision and help the customer reach a confident choice.

The takeaway

Industrial sales are rarely won through one relationship alone. They are won by addressing the needs of the people who collectively make the decision.
