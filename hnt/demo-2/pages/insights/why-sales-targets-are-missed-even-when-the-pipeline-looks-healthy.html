Why Sales Targets Are Missed Even When the Pipeline Looks Healthy

Ajit Jain · 2 min read

A sales pipeline may show many opportunities and a large value, yet the final orders do not arrive. This often happens when the pipeline measures optimism rather than evidence. Opportunities are entered too early, expected dates keep moving and the same business is counted as likely to close month after month.

Review the quality of each opportunity. Is there a clear customer need? Has the salesperson met the relevant decision-makers? Is there a defined budget, trial or approval process? What is the next action, who owns it and by when? An opportunity without a customer-confirmed next step may not be as strong as the forecast suggests.

Compare conversion rates at each stage and look at how long opportunities remain open. Remove or reclassify business that has no realistic progress. Encourage honest forecasting rather than rewarding inflated numbers.

A healthy pipeline is not simply large. It is current, evidence-based and balanced across customers and stages.

The takeaway

Better forecasting begins with better conversations about what is actually happening inside each opportunity.
