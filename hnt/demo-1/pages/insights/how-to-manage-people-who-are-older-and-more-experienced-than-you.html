Managing Sales Teams

How to Manage People Who Are Older and More Experienced Than You

By Ajit Jain · 2 min read

Being promoted to lead people who are older and more experienced can feel uncomfortable. You may wonder whether they will accept your authority or quietly question your decisions. The answer is not to prove that you know more than they do. It is to show that you can help the team succeed.

Start by listening. Understand each person's experience, customer relationships and strengths. Acknowledge what they know, and involve them when decisions affect their area. At the same time, be clear about priorities, standards and accountability. Respect does not mean avoiding difficult conversations.

Do not compete with experienced team members or try to demonstrate authority in every meeting. Set direction, remove obstacles and give credit where it is due. Over time, people are more likely to respect a manager who is fair, consistent and dependable than one who relies only on designation.

The takeawayThe practical lesson is simple: you do not need to be the most experienced person in the room. You need to help the experience in the room work together.

— Ajit
