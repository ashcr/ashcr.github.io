Winning Customers

How to Win Back a Customer You Lost Three Years Ago

By Ajit Jain · 2 min read

Winning back a lost customer requires more than sending a new quotation. The customer may remember a quality failure, a delivery problem, poor service or a relationship that broke down. Even if people have changed, the organisation's history may still influence the decision.

Begin by understanding why the business was lost. Speak to people who know the account, review old complaints and examine what has changed since then. Be honest about past shortcomings. Avoid blaming the customer or the previous salesperson.

Look for a genuine reason to reconnect. Perhaps your product, service capability or supply arrangement has improved. A new application or a change in the customer's requirements may also create an opening. Offer a low-risk way to demonstrate the difference, with clear measures and close support.

Do not expect the customer to restore the old business immediately. Rebuild confidence through small commitments delivered consistently.

The takeawayA lost customer can become an important source of learning. Winning them back is possible when the supplier shows that it has changed, not merely that it wants another order.

— Ajit
